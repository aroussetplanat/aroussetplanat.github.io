abstract: "Drawing on 100 million transactions, we show how speculators, hedgers, and market makers interact in the world’s largest foreign exchange (FX) derivatives market and that derivatives trading affects exchange rates. Firms in the largest client sectors—that is, pension and investment funds, insurers, and nonfinancials—maintain persistent net-short dollar derivatives exposures, with dealer banks providing the liquidity. Hedge funds, with comparatively smaller net exposures, adjust positions in line with speculative investment strategies. While dealer banks insulate themselves from changes in speculative demand by taking offsetting positions with hedgers, especially nonfinancials, nonbank market makers absorb residual exposures “on the margin.” Hedge funds’ flows help transmit monetary policy shocks to exchange rates, while investment funds’ ‘flight-to-safety’ contribute to dollar appreciations when credit risk rises. Overall, exchange rates depend on the composition of trading activities in FX derivative markets."
